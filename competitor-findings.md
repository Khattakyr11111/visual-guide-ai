# Competitor-informed product findings

## Be My Eyes

The official Be My Eyes site positions the product around connecting blind or low-vision users to volunteers and companies through live video and AI. It emphasizes real-time assistance, worldwide availability, anonymity, and AI plus human help. The product is strongest when the user needs a person or general visual description.

Source: https://www.bemyeyes.com/

## Google Lookout

The Google Play listing describes seven modes: Text, Documents, Explore, Currency, Food labels, Find, and Images. Lookout uses computer vision and generative AI to read, identify, find, describe, and answer questions about visual content. The listing also notes that Images mode description and Q&A are globally available in English, while other modes support many languages.

Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.accessibility.reveal&hl=en_US

## Differentiation selected for Visual Guide AI

Visual Guide AI should not try to win by duplicating every recognition mode. Its differentiator is a movement-safety layer: Safe-Step Confidence combines camera cues, motion/orientation signals, temporary location sharing, concise speech, haptic reinforcement, and a deliberate pause-and-recheck rule. The product should distinguish information from instruction, state uncertainty, and avoid promising that a path is safe.

The first implementation should expose:

1. A first-run helper setup that configures speech language and permissions before the blind user begins.
2. A one-hour trusted-guardian share session with visible countdown and stop control.
3. A Safe-Step state model: clear, notice, caution, urgent, and re-check.
4. Spoken cues in English, Spanish, Hindi, and Urdu, with a concise mode.
5. Camera capture for scene/product questions, routed through the server-side multimodal model rather than exposing credentials on-device.
6. A clear safety boundary that the app remains an awareness aid and does not replace a cane, guide dog, or trained human support.
